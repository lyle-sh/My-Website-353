“I would prefer to do this two days in a row than just drive my 296 on a track day.”

While initially 8 curated laps and 2 hot laps may seem expensive, if you consider the infrastructure behind it — 8 laps driving around a proper circuit with a very accomplished race driver with a whole race team behind you, so you get new slicks bolted on and everything is checked after every session — it was not at all expensive. We also got to meet the two owners of Lyle's team, who were there — top gents — and the mechanics who sorted out our cars were very professional and nice blokes.

Read more Read less

If you have driven a 458 or a 488, getting into a 488 Challenge car and just setting off is very easy. It feels very familiar. The key difference is not the speed of the car — it doesn't feel any faster than a 488 GTB. It is the fact that the grip and brakes totally change what you can do. Slicks and a proper race suspension and brakes made me approach it too tentatively. The car will pounce like a cat almost without breaking traction, allowing you to do insane things that would just utterly humiliate you in a road car.

I think I am spoiled now for road cars. I would prefer to do this two days in a row than just drive my 296 on a track day. If I was to ship my 296 to a decent track and pay for consumables with no race team helping me, it might end badly and expensively and at best cost not much less.

And the hot laps — kind of like a revelation. Don't turn up hungover in any way for a hot lap. It is immensely entertaining but when you are not in control and just experiencing it, the violent forces are more discombobulating and it is more so in a race car driven by a proper professional.

Do it. You will not go to your deathbed saying, ‘I regret that track day at Cremona.’

Julien
Ferrari 458 & 296 owner
